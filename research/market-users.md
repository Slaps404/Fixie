**Market and user case: "Open repair for everyone" (as of Oct 24, 2026)**

Caveat: several figures came from secondary sites, and I marked those below. Verify the "verify" items before they go on a slide.

**Problem statement:** When an appliance dies, a non-technical senior faces three bad choices. They can pay $100-$400 for a technician, take a chance on a stranger at the door, or throw out a machine that needed a $15-$45 part. We give them a phone number that fixes it by voice and sources the part.

### Users and phone-first behavior
- **Aging:** 18.9% of the US is 65+ today, projected to reach 23.4% by 2060. The world share is projected to go from 10.5% to 19.6% ([Census, Aug/Sep 2026](https://www.census.gov/newsroom/press-releases/2026/older-adult-population-projections.html)). About 64M US seniors is my own estimate (18.9% of roughly 340M), not a sourced figure.
- **Smartphone gap:** Pew 2025 puts smartphone ownership at 78% for 65+, versus 90-97% for younger groups ([Pew tech adoption](https://www.pewresearch.org/technology/2025/tech-adoption)). I saw the 78% only through secondary sites, so verify it. About 1 in 5 seniors has no smartphone, and owning one does not mean comfort with apps.
- **Channel preference:** Only 35% of people aged 45-75 prefer digital over phone for service questions, versus 53% of 18-44 ([Verint via eMarketer](https://www.emarketer.com/content/older-adults-prefer-phone-calls-digital-customer-service), 2023, directional). Older consumers also distrust chatbots and in-app messaging ([Twilio via Computer Weekly](https://www.computerweekly.com/news/252523867/Older-customers-prefer-phone-calls-to-chatbots)).
- **Tech trend:** AARP's 2025 Tech Trends report says texting is the primary way adults 50+ communicate, and their AI use rose from 18% to 30% ([Senior Housing News summary](https://seniorhousingnews.com/?p=52289)). Voice, text and photo are the right inputs.

### Repair economics
- **Technician cost:** Appliance repair averages $179 (range $108-$250) ([HomeAdvisor](https://www.homeadvisor.com/cost/kitchens/repair-an-appliance)). HVAC repair averages $350 (range $100-$3,000), and service-call fees alone run $100-$250 ([HomeAdvisor HVAC](https://www.homeadvisor.com/cost/heating-and-cooling/repair-an-hvac-system)).
- **DIY part cost:**
  - An AC capacitor part costs $8-$45, against $150-$400 installed by a pro ([This Old House](https://www.thisoldhouse.com/heating-cooling/ac-capacitor-cost), [HomeAdvisor](https://www.homeadvisor.com/cost/heating-and-cooling/replace-ac-capacitor)).
  - Pros charge $100-$212 to replace the indoor fan capacitor, which costs about $9-$12.
  - Safety caveat: the capacitor holds high voltage, and This Old House advises a pro. For the demo, stick to safe fixes such as filters, breakers, door gaskets, drain pumps, mower spark plugs and carburetor cleaning, with a "stop, call a pro" guardrail.
- **Repair vs replace:** The "50% rule" says replace when the repair costs more than half the price of a new unit. AARP says to base it on current replacement price. Typical lifespans are 9 years for a dishwasher, 10 for a washer and 13 for a fridge ([NerdWallet](https://www.nerdwallet.com/finance/learn/when-to-repair-or-replace-your-appliances)). Consumer Reports now bases its advice on 528,000 owner-reported products ([Consumer Reports](https://www.consumerreports.org/home-garden/lawn-mowers/should-you-repair-or-replace-your-broken-lawn-mower-a9568997026)).
- **Mowers:** Repairs run about $40-$200, and average mower life is about 8.5 years ([Mowrator](https://mowrator.com/blogs/lawn-mower/repair-or-replace)). I found no reliable national count of mowers discarded each year. Do not invent one.
- **Supply side:**
  - US appliance repair is about a $7.0B industry with 37,769 businesses ([IBISWorld](https://www.ibisworld.com/united-states/market-size/appliance-repair/1710)).
  - A trade source reports a 19% technician gap and an average technician age of 47 ([ServiceMag](https://www.servicemag.org/news/technician-workforce-shortage-2026)). Other sources put the unfilled count much lower, at 10-15K.
- **Parts aftermarket:** Estimates vary by definition.
  - Emergen Research puts home appliance parts and accessories at about $45.7B in 2024, with replacement parts about 60% of that ([Emergen](https://www.emergenresearch.com/index.php/industry-report/home-appliance-parts-accessories-market)).
  - A wider accessories definition gives $122.5B ([Verified Market Research](https://www.verifiedmarketresearch.com/product/home-appliance-accessories-market/)).
  - Call it "tens of billions" and cite both.

### Waste and right to repair
- **E-waste:** The world generated about 62M tonnes in 2022, and only 22.3% was documented as recycled. Small equipment is the largest category at about 20.4B kg, with a 12% documented recycling rate ([Global E-waste Monitor 2024 via Embedded Artistry](https://embeddedartistry.com/blog/category/waste-reduction/), [The Register](https://www.theregister.com/2024/03/21/ewaste_grows/)).
- **Right to repair:** About 25.75% of Americans live in a state with an enforceable electronics right-to-repair law as of Jan 1, 2026. Connecticut and Texas laws push that to at least 35.5% by fall 2026 ([PIRG](https://pirg.org/articles/more-than-one-quarter-of-americans-covered-by-right-to-repair-come-jan-1/)). These laws cover electronics, not appliances. The honest framing is that the law is opening manuals and parts, and no one is yet serving the people who can't use them. PIRG also notes many companies still don't support repair.

### Trust and scams
- **FTC 60+ fraud:** Reported losses were about $3.0B in 2025, up from about $600M in 2020. Total fraud losses across all ages hit a record $15.9B ([FTC older adults report, Dec 2025](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2025/12/ftc-issues-annual-report-congress-agencys-actions-protect-older-adults), [PPC Land summary](https://ppc.land/older-americans-lost-3-billion-to-fraud-in-2025-ftc-tells-congress/)).
- **Home repair scams:** About 83,000 home repair, improvement and product scam reports went to the FTC in 2023 (secondary source, verify). A Florida State study found that in a retirement community the most common fraud source was home services, especially unnecessary repairs ([JLC](https://www.jlconline.com/business/florida-state-university-releases-new-report-on-elderly-fraud_o/)). The usual tactic is a small visible fix followed by an inflated "it's worse than expected" bill.
- **Pitch angle:** Our agent has no commission, never asks for a payment card up front, and tells you plainly when a pro is needed.

### Health stakes
- A CDC analysis of 2012 heat deaths found 69% occurred at home, and 91% of those homes lacked working AC ([CDC MMWR via AAFP](https://www.aafp.org/afp/2005/0901/p951a.html)). The data is old, so verify.
- Older adults accounted for 39% of US heat deaths, which rose 117% from 1999 to 2023 ([Skilled Nursing News](https://skillednursingnews.com/?p=43250)).

### "1 billion people" framing
- 3.1B people live under mobile coverage but don't use mobile internet. About 1B of them own a device and still don't use it for internet ([GSMA 2025](https://gsma.com/newsroom/press-release/gsma-calls-for-renewed-focus-on-closing-the-usage-gap-as-more-than-3-billion-people-remain-offline-despite-available-mobile-internet-services/)).
- Feature phones are 60% of mobile internet subscriber devices in Sub-Saharan Africa and 55% of Q3 2024 African phone shipments ([Techloy](https://techloy.com/chart-feature-phones-accounted-for-55-percent-of-all-mobile-phone-shipments-in-africa-in-q3-2024)).
- A Toxics Link study of Indian cities found lower-income households repair mainly for cost reasons, and the barriers are parts shortages and untrained technicians ([Toxics Link 2026](https://toxicslink.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/Electronic%20Repair%20report.pdf)).
- A voice call works on any phone, so it reaches both the US senior and the feature-phone user in Lagos or Kolkata.

### Personas
- **Dolores, 74, Phoenix:** Her AC dies in a 112°F heatwave. She lives alone, is wary of strangers, and has a flip phone for calls. She calls the number, and the agent walks her through checking the breaker and the thermostat batteries. The agent diagnoses a likely capacitor, finds a $12 part, and books a verified local tech for the install only. She pays a fraction of the quote, with no door-knocker.
- **Ray, 68, Ohio:** A retired machinist whose mower won't start, with a repair shop wait of several weeks. He texts a photo and the agent identifies the carburetor. It talks him through cleaning it, then calls three parts distributors, finds the cheapest gasket kit, and phones him back with the total and the delivery date.
- **Amina, 41, Lagos:** She sells at a market stall, owns a feature phone, and her fridge compressor relay fails. She calls the number in Hausa or English. The agent walks her through the diagnosis, then calls parts dealers to find the cheapest relay. It reports the price by voice, with no app and no data plan.
