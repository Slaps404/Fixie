Moat: things no LLM has

  • Parts graph: model number → exact part → cross-refs → live price and stock, learned from every supplier call. The next lookup is instant.
  • Confirmed fixes: which repair actually worked for this model with this symptom, verified on the follow-up call.
  • Asset memory: it knows your appliances and calls first about recalls and failures.
  • Buyer's agent: no commissions, no upsell. The opposite of a home-repair scam.

One engine, two markets

  • Consumer wedge: seniors and non-technical people. Home-warranty companies and insurers pay per resolved case.
  • Ports and fleets: a fault code triggers sourcing before the berth window. Crane downtime costs an estimated $5k–50k+/hr.
  • Defense facilities: base HVAC uses off-the-shelf parts, no ITAR. The Army created an HVAC task force on Oct 15, 2026.
  • Multi-site facilities: restaurant equipment downtime costs a large operator about $18M/yr.

Guardrails (said on stage)

  • Every call opens with "I'm an AI assistant; this call is recorded" (FCC 24-17, CA §632).
  • Safety tiers: DIY · DIY with caution · pro required (gas, refrigerant, high voltage).
  • Caps enforced in code: 8 calls, 20 minutes, $40, approved vendors only.
  • Voicemail, IVR loop, or 90s of silence → skip. Cap hit → stop and call the user.
  • Nothing is bought without a spoken yes. Full transcript audit log.
  • Honest pitch: compares quotes for the identical part, not "negotiates".
